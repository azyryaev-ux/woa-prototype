About this report

In a matter of years, the ownership of American rental housing has shifted dramatically. A small number of corporations and institutional investors now control hundreds of thousands of homes across the country, reshaping communities, driving rents, and making it harder than ever for ordinary Americans to find affordable places to live.

This report draws on property records, corporate filings, and LLC network analysis across 9 cities to trace who really owns the roof over your head.

Ownership concentration
Who holds the housing stock?

Across our nine metro areas, nearly half of all rental units are controlled by large-portfolio landlords - those owning 100 or more units. This share has risen steadily since 2010, with the sharpest acceleration coming after 2020. Small-scale "mom and pop" landlords, once the backbone of the rental market, now hold a shrinking slice of the pie.

The six biggest corporate owners

Six companies together control over 330,000 rental units across the cities in our dataset. Most are backed by institutional investors - asset managers and private equity funds - whose names rarely appear on lease agreements. Their rapid growth since 2012 has reshaped entire neighborhoods in cities from Houston to Boston.
